The Mountain Range

Sierra Madre: A biodiversity hotspot worth protecting.

The Sierra Madre Mountain Range is the longest in the Philippines, stretching over 500 km from Cagayan to Quezon. It is called the country’s “green wall” because it shields lowland areas from typhoons and floods while serving as a biodiversity hotspot and home to indigenous groups (Rappler, 2023).

We support locally led conservation, safeguard critical ecosystems, and help communities build a future where people and nature thrive together.

The pressure points

What puts the Sierra Madre at risk?

The range faces overlapping pressures from development, resource extraction, and weak protection of its forests and communities.

  1. Large-scale miningLegal operations like Dinapigue Mining Corporation in Isabela have caused massive forest clearing in protected zones (Inquirer, 2024).
  2. Infrastructure projectsHydropower dams such as Kaliwa and Ahunan, along with coastal developments, threaten habitats and displace communities (Earth Journalism, 2023).
  3. Deforestation from loggingLegal and illegal logging continue despite the range being declared protected (DENR-FMB, 2020).
  4. Slash-and-burn farmingUpland farming, known as kaingin, leads to forest loss and soil degradation (Rappler, 2023).
  5. Land conversionExpanding settlements and road projects cut through forest areas (Rappler, 2023).
  6. Weak enforcement of protection lawsDespite protected-area status, permits are still granted for extractive activities (Brigada News, 2024).

The consequences

What happens when the forest is lost?

Damage to Sierra Madre reaches far beyond the forest, affecting wildlife, communities, safety, water and the economy.

01

Loss of biodiversity

Endangered species such as the Philippine eagle, cloud rat, and golden-crowned flying fox lose their habitats due to deforestation and mining (Rappler, 2023).

01

Wildlife at risk

Habitat loss pushes endangered species closer to extinction and weakens the forest’s living web.

02

Increased disaster risk

Forest destruction weakens Sierra Madre's natural barrier, exposing Metro Manila and Central Luzon to strong typhoons and floods (Rolling Stone PH, 2024).

02

Stronger storms

When the forest is gone, flood and wind risks rise sharply for nearby communities.

03

Displacement of indigenous communities

Dumagat-Remontado and other groups are forced to leave ancestral lands due to mining and dam projects (Earth Journalism Network, 2023).

03

Communities uprooted

Development without consent can displace families and erase cultural ties to the land.

04

Soil erosion and landslides

Tree removal from mining and logging areas increases erosion, landslide risk, and river sedimentation (Global Forest Watch, 2024).

04

Fragile slopes

Without roots holding the soil, mountain slopes become unstable during rain.

05

Water contamination

Mining runoff and dam construction affect rivers and groundwater, threatening freshwater supply (PCIJ, 2024).

05

Clean water at risk

Forest damage can pollute streams and reduce safe freshwater access for communities.

06

Climate vulnerability

Loss of forests reduces carbon absorption, worsening the effects of climate change in Luzon (Climate Change Commission, 2022).

06

Forest as climate shield

Healthy forests store carbon and help buffer the impacts of a warming climate.

07

Economic impacts

Deforestation-driven flooding, soil degradation, and sedimentation affect downstream agriculture and fisheries (PCIJ, 2024).

07

Costs beyond the forest

When ecosystems fail, farms, fisheries, and local livelihoods lose long-term stability.

Current status

Mining operations in Isabela (Dinapigue) are legally allowed until 2032 (Inquirer, 2024).

The Kaliwa Dam project threatens to submerge 9,700 hectares of forests and displace 1,485 families in Tanay, Rizal, and General Nakar and Infanta, Quezon.

Large parts of the forest are already cleared; satellite images show widespread loss (Brigada News, 2024).

Indigenous groups continue to resist displacement from ancestral lands (Rappler, 2023).

Existing solution

Reforestation, rooted in community.

Reforestation programs including the National Greening Program and local DENR initiatives plant trees where forests have been damaged, degraded, or lost.

Current status

Work is ongoing

Tree-planting efforts continue through cooperation among private organizations, environmental groups, local communities, and academic institutions such as the University of the Philippines Los Baños.

Procedure / program

Plant, restore, care

  • DENR, LGUs, NGOs, and schools organize tree planting.
  • Community participation is encouraged.
  • Upland forest restoration works alongside coastal efforts, including mangrove and coral rehabilitation.
  • UPLB Land Grant Forester Rey Lorida oversaw the activity, with staff member Rose Bocado facilitating proper planting techniques to support seedling survival.
Effect / result

Restoring more than trees

Reforestation addresses tree loss and helps restore natural systems that protect communities and wildlife in Sierra Madre, Nat-Re and FEED initiative shows how cooperation, native tree planting, and community involvement can contribute to environmental restoration.

Reflection

Why I chose reforestation

After researching three possible solutions, I decided that reforestation is the most effective and sustainable approach in restoring the Sierra Madre. Although a law has already been enacted to support environmental protection, its effectiveness depends on consistent monitoring and proper implementation. Meanwhile, the construction of the Kaliwa Dam continues to move forward, with a target completion date, despite opposition from environmentalists and Indigenous communities. For this reason, I chose reforestation because I believe that even small initiatives can create a significant impact when carried out consistently, whether quarterly or annually. By encouraging greater participation from both the public and private sectors, these individual efforts can gradually develop into a larger environmental movement. Through sustained commitment, collective action, and long-term dedication, reforestation can become a meaningful crusade to restore, preserve, and protect the Sierra Madre for future generations.
